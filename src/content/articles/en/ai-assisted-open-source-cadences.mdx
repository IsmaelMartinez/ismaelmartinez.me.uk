## From One Project to Several

Maintaining one open-source project well is a steady, long-lived cadence: a single codebase, a single rhythm. Running several at once is a different problem. AI assistance makes it feasible to keep several projects moving in parallel, at different speeds, and that changes what the workflow has to handle, because no single cadence fits them all.

## Five Cadences

[teams-for-linux](https://github.com/IsmaelMartinez/teams-for-linux) is the long-lived one: eight years of history, hundreds of thousands of active users, the full harness.

[bonnie-wee-plot](https://github.com/IsmaelMartinez/bonnie-wee-plot) is seasonal, a Scottish allotment app where the features that matter in March (sowing windows) aren't the ones that matter in August (harvest, blight watch). The codebase grows in chapters, and it's the gardening year that decides what to build next.

[votescot](https://github.com/IsmaelMartinez/votescot) was ephemeral, an open-source vote compass built for the May 2026 Scottish Parliament election. The relevance window was narrow: the code had to be solid for as long as it lasted, and the lifecycle came with a built-in end. The election has passed, the scheduled data syncs are off and the repository is archived, with the site left up as a read-only record. That is what an ephemeral project is supposed to look like afterwards.

[wifisentinel](https://github.com/IsmaelMartinez/wifisentinel) is reactive, built quickly because a specific situation needed it (a dodgy Amsterdam Airbnb where I wanted to know what was actually on the network). What started as a one-off grew into a full toolkit once the original problem was solved: compliance scoring, RF analysis, external reconnaissance of a domain, scan history and a dashboard.

[PR-Agent](https://github.com/the-pr-agent/pr-agent) is inherited: Qodo's open-source code reviewer, donated to the community in 2026 with 13,000 stars and years of history I had no part in, which I now help maintain. The cadence is set by other people's pull requests and by a codebase I am still learning, so the job is mostly review, release and saying no, on code that was never mine. That one deserves its own article.

## Match the Harness to the Cadence

The full harness is right for an eight-year project and excessive for a one-month vote compass. Putting the whole process on an ephemeral codebase is bureaucracy dressed up as rigour. But "less harness" isn't "no harness": votescot had CI, a linter, and manual review on every diff to the end. The standards that protect users from something breaking apply at every cadence; the ones that protect a long-lived codebase from future-self confusion aren't needed when there's no future self to confuse. The discipline is in sizing the harness to the lifecycle, not in applying maximum ceremony everywhere.

## The Death Star Is Still Real

Bugs hide in complexity. AI doesn't override that rule; it makes it more pressing. When the cost of writing code drops, only the writing cost drops, not the reading, debugging, or retirement cost. The temptation to keep adding is greater than ever, so saying "no, that's a Death Star" matters more, not less. It shows up differently at each cadence: "ten years of maintenance" on a long-lived project, "too clever for six weeks" on an ephemeral one. But it's the same reminder.

## Signals Are the Constraint

With AI assistance and a right-sized harness, the bottleneck shifts. It's no longer how much code you can ship; it's whether you can tell, in time, when something has gone wrong. Tests, CI, manual review and the ADRs are all signal: the harness exists to produce it and the workflow exists to act on it, the same loop [the workflow piece](/en/articles/ai-assisted-open-source-workflow) described. AI accelerates the cycles, so signal quality has to keep up. Skimp there and AI stops amplifying your productivity and starts amplifying the chaos.

## Closing Thought

Same discipline, very different lifecycles. The harness bends to the cadence, and signal is what you optimise for. This isn't settled: new cadences will show up that don't fit these five, and the Death Star will keep being a temptation.
