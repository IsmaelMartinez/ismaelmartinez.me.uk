# Article-voice skill and AI-OSS series polish

Goal: a personal `article-voice` skill that keeps the trilingual articles in Ismael's own voice (Spanish composed first, translated to English and Catalan) and free of AI-writing tells, then a loop applying it to the four draft AI-OSS articles until they are ready to publish.

Decisions taken 2026-07-31: the skill lives in `~/.claude-home/skills/article-voice/` (personal, usable outside this repo; not committed here). Its scope is all four modes: drafting and translating, a de-AI audit mode (report only, no rewriting), an exemplar library of verbatim quotes, and short-form posts (LinkedIn/Medium/dev.to teasers).

Branch correction, 2026-08-01: the original intent was to land this alongside PR #105, but that PR was squash-merged as `855d243c` on 2026-07-29 and its branch deleted, so all four articles now sit on `main` still carrying `draft: true`. This plan and the article-loop work therefore ride on a fresh branch, `article-voice-skill`, cut from current `main`. The recurring "Reflexión final" closer keeps its heading as a column signature, but the "restate the thesis as an aphorism" formula inside it is treated as a tell to vary.

Decision 2026-08-01, published articles: do not rewrite them for voice. Three categories are treated differently. A defect gets fixed silently, because it is maintenance and not editing (the orphan caption still live on `opentelemetry-signals` is the only known one, folded into G8 as a one-line cleanup). A house-style change gets applied freely, with precedent already set by commit `2e5a8e93`, which scrubbed 62 em-dashes across 16 published articles. A voice rewrite does not happen. The reasoning: no reader ever complained, the tells only surfaced under adversarial statistical analysis, and they are audible mainly when four pieces are read in sequence, which is the unpublished series' problem and not a standalone published piece's. The published pair also has a better use than repair. `the-ai-automation-trap` is the best negative exemplar available (ideas genuinely his, prose that is not) and `the-death-ish-of-logs` is the best positive one, so both become the calibration corpus for G3 rather than fix targets. Caveat kept on the record: the Trap is the keystone all four drafts cross-link to, so it gets a reread before the series ships, not a rewrite.

Evidence base: a parallel dossier sweep (run `wf_f9fdaaae-5dc`) over the nine published English articles, the five Spanish sources, real teams-for-linux issue replies, the teams-for-linux-issue-review skill (which already encodes the conversational register: short, hedged verbs, no em-dashes, "I hope helps!" / "Thanks again!" sign-offs, cut restating tails), and whatever public web presence is reachable (Medium, dev.to, LinkedIn best-effort). Includes the sidequest: identify which published article got "a bit too much AI", adversarially verified.

### G1 findings summary

Three corrections to my prior assumptions. The published corpus contains no Death Star; the actual recurring prop is user 1234 buying 7 bananas, with a service called Bananaitor. Articles almost always close by asking the reader a real question, and the two most machine-flavoured pieces are precisely the ones that drop it. The biggest surprise for the article loop: the voice leaks in the English translation, not in the Spanish. Concrete repairs, mostly in bot-to-skill: "because… because not" is a calque and not English (wants "because… just because"); "Only time will tell" is a stock cliché standing in for the plainer "eso solo se ve con el tiempo"; the "meh" scare quotes soften a fully naturalised noun; "After all" formalises the shrug in "Si total"; "spending weeks" and "confuse" flatten "tirarte semanas" and "no te lías". Also `su final de fábrica` lost its factory image as "a built-in end". Catalan preserves the voice better than English because the idiom stock overlaps. Copyedit item: Spanish gender wobbles between "los issues" and "las issues" inside a single file, normalise to "las issues". Keep the en-only "harness" rendering of "restricciones" in cadences, it disambiguates better than the Spanish source.

### G2 findings summary

The skill was written, then tested by five agents that could read only the skill files. Three tests passed: audit mode produced 17 findings on `cadences.es` and rewrote nothing; the translator independently produced "because… just because" and "one of those meh ones" with no scare quotes; the drafter produced two Spanish paragraphs on the allotment app that read like him. A held-out ranking test agreed with the dossier on the most human article but swapped the middle two, so scores should be treated as ordinal rather than exact.

An adversarial critic found three critical defects, all verified by hand against the corpus before acting. First, the skill asserted that the reader-question closer was dropped by the two most machine-flavoured pieces, which the evidence falsifies: `the-ai-automation-trap` closes with "I'd love to hear how you're navigating it", and only four of nine published articles close on a question at all, so the rule banned the majority behaviour. Replaced with the three closings actually in use, question, deferral, or tie-back, and a ban only on the neat aphorism with nothing after it. Second, the calibration section named both test articles and their expected verdicts inside the always-loaded path, so the checklist could never fail its own test; the answer key moved to `references/calibration.md` with an instruction not to open it until an audit is written. Third, drafting and auditing carried incompatible antithesis budgets (one per article versus two per 1000 words); now a single number, one pivot per 1000 body words.

Five majors also closed. `Reflexión final` appears in zero published articles (they use Conclusion four times, plus What now?, What comes next?, Looking for feedback, A Closing Thought), so it is scoped to this series and must not be retrofitted; the owner's decision to keep the heading and vary the shape stands within that scope. The banned-word list included four words he has published (robust twice, crucial, landscape, actionable insights), one of them inside a sentence the exemplars quote as a model hedge, so bans are now two-tier. The ellipsis rule prescribed a per-article quota the corpus does not support (nine of thirteen English files carry none) and would have normalised stet-protected ASCII dots in the Spanish drafts. The LinkedIn trigger promised work the skill then refused, now bounded to one labelled guess. And the skill was overwhelmingly prohibitive, 24 "never" lines against five rules of construction with no paragraph-length model at all, so v2 adds a worked unit from bot-to-skill annotated sentence by sentence, the corpus shape numbers (626 to 1868 body words, four to twelve `##`), the closing tie-back as a named move, and title and heading conventions.

A second round (run `wf_4b9bb1d8-56c`) re-audited v2 and tested the answer-key split. The split works: the blind auditor never opened `calibration.md` before finishing, and independently reached the same verdict on the Trap with matching numbers (14 pivots against the recorded 13, 21.4 per 1000 against 20, mean paragraph 15.2 against 14.7).
